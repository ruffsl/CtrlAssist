# Motion sensors and the touchpad

## Goal

A PlayStation pad has three kinds of input beyond its buttons, sticks and triggers: a touchpad that reports where fingers are, a gyroscope that reports how fast the pad is turning, and an accelerometer that reports which way is down and how the pad is shaken. The kernel gives each its own node beside the pad's. CtrlAssist reads none of them, the touchpad's click among them: the kernel's PlayStation driver reports the click on the touchpad's node, not the pad's. A virtual DualSense has a touchpad and sensors that report nothing, and a click it never presses.

That was a choice of scope: the routing model is built for controls that rest at zero. It has become a loss. A hidden pad now has every one of its nodes hidden, so that a game sees one controller and not a second, partial one; with that, a game can no longer read the real pad's sensors or touchpad for itself, and nothing carries them to the virtual pad. While a setup hides a PlayStation pad, gyro aiming, tilt and touchpad gestures are gone.

The goal is for a virtual DualSense to carry them, by rules that keep what each mode promises: the primary player stays in charge, and what the assist does is bounded in time and in scope.

## State

The research is in, the spike has been run, the build steps are written, and the maintainer has answered every open question but one (all 2026-11-23).

- **The hidden pad's touchpad kept from the compositor: done,** before 0.5.0 by the maintainer's decision, for every hidden pad. The rule sets `LIBINPUT_IGNORE_DEVICE=1` on every event node it hides, and has udev tell its listeners that a touchpad hidden late, by a change event, went and came back, since libinput reads the property only as it adds a node and ignores change events (read in libinput 1.31's `src/udev-seat.c`). The hider check, with cage as the seat's compositor and libinput's reader running throughout: a fake DualSense's touchpad is listed by libinput and held by cage while shown; hidden, it carries the property, libinput's tools don't list it, the reader leaves it alone (`event10: device is ignored`) and cage holds none of it; plugged in while hidden, the reader and cage took it as it was added, all seven times it was tried before the rule sent the remove and the add, and with them they let it go and leave it alone; shown again, the property is gone, it is listed, and cage holds it. On hardware, still to be seen: KWin's list of input devices with no touchpad for a hidden DualSense.
- **Motion and touch in the pad state: done,** before 0.5.0 by the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-23, where the plan first put the build after it. The core's pad state carries the motion sensors' last reading and the touchpad's two contacts beside its controls, and the touchpad's click as `gamepad::TOUCHPAD`; a mux takes both blocks whole from its first input, or from the input selected where it selects, and a demux sends them with the rest. Nothing reads or writes motion or contacts yet, so no running setup changes. The count of readings and the interval, which the step proposed, are built as proposed (a wrapping `u32` count, and microseconds by the pad's clock) and await the maintainer's word; where the build departs from the step is under Deviations.
- **The rest: not begun.** The virtual DualSense's step is next, once what gates it under Still to find out is known. What is under Still to find out comes first, each item before the step it gates. The one open question, showing who has the motion, bears on no step yet written.

## What the modes promise

From the 0.4.0 README, which is where the modes were first described, and which the rewrite keeps:

- **Priority.** The assist overrides a control while it is active on it, past a deadzone, and the control returns to the primary when the assist lets go. "Partial and asynchronous assistance."
- **Average.** The two blend while both are active, and otherwise whoever is active is heard. "Cooperative input and subtle corrections."
- **Toggle.** Everything comes from the pad that has control, and a button hands over. "Fine-grain conflict-free control."

The maintainer's account of what an assist is for: the helping player supplements the primary player's fine motor skills, and some modes respect the primary player's autonomy by keeping interventions temporal and scoped. A rule for motion or touch is right if it reads as the same promise.

The same README says more that bears on it. Who it is for: "partial asynchronous input, i.e. offloading camera angle management, movements requiring speed and precision, or on standby backup during difficult combat encounters". Managing the camera is the first thing named, and where a game aims by gyro the camera is the gyro: an assist who takes the camera over wants the turning, which the first position below allows in priority and toggle. Why a pad isn't simply passed back and forth: handing over breaks immersion, adds friction, and costs the primary player agency ("waiting for returned control") and learning ("no haptic feedback hinders recognizing cues"). So the primary player goes on holding their own pad and feeling it throughout, and whatever is done with motion must not ask them to put it down. And what 0.4.0 did not do: "tactile triggers, gyroscopic and accelerometer motion tracking, or more exotic force feedback waveforms are not yet supported". Motion was never carried; whether a game could still read a hidden pad's sensors for itself under 0.4.0's ways of hiding has not been checked.

Its Background names what the feature is modeled on, which the research is to read for what each does with motion and touch: Controller Assist (Copilot) on Xbox and Windows, Second Controller Assistance on PlayStation, and InputPlumber, an input router for Linux that makes one virtual pad of several devices, a handheld's separate motion sensor and touchpads among them.

## Decisions (first position)

**The touchpad follows the mode's own rule.** A touchpad with no finger on it is at rest, so it is a control like a stick: in priority the assist's touch overrides while a finger is down, in average the nearest thing to a blend is to be decided (two fingers' positions don't average into a third that means anything), in toggle it is the active pad's. Its click is already a button. (Not so for a pad of the kernel's PlayStation driver, which reports the click on the touchpad's node: see What the spike found.)

**The gyroscope's rate follows the mode's own rule too.** A pad held still, or lying on a lap or a table, turns at a rate near zero, so a rate is a control that rests at zero, with a deadzone as a stick has: in priority the assist's turning overrides while it exceeds the deadzone, in average the two rates blend while both do, in toggle it is the active pad's. The deadzone wants a default that clears a hand's tremor, and to be a profile's value as a stick's is.

**The accelerometer does not rest, and is not blended.** It reports gravity, which is never zero, and two pads' gravity averaged is a shorter vector pointing nowhere real. In toggle it is the active pad's. Outside toggle it is undecided; the first position is the primary's, always: the primary's pad stays the truth of which way is down, the assist helps through turning alone, and where a game estimates the pad's pose from both sensors, its own correction for drift brings the estimate back to the primary's pose after the assist lets go. What was decided is under Decided after the research.

**One source pad has nothing to decide.** In unicast and multicast there is one pad, and its touchpad and sensors go where its other controls go.

**Only a virtual DualSense can carry them.** The other identities have no touchpad and no sensors to report through.

## What the research found

Three surveys were made on 2026-11-23, by agents reading public sources. What a source says is given with the source; what is reasoned from it is marked as inference. None of it has been tried here on a pad.

### The gyroscope, and aiming by it

**A game integrates a rate.** Aiming by gyro is `camera.yaw += rate.yaw × sensitivity × dt`, and the same for pitch: a relative device, as a mouse is ([GyroWiki, "The Gyro is a Mouse"](http://gyrowiki.jibbsmart.com/blog:good-gyro-controls-part-1:the-gyro-is-a-mouse)). The camera's angle is continuous whatever the rate's source, and no absolute pose of the pad maps to where the camera points. Sensitivity is in real turning to turning in the game: at 1, 37.5° of the pad is 37.5° of the camera. Steam's guidance to developers is to add up every source of camera turning before moving the camera, and not to take the last one active ([Steam Deck recommendations](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamdeck/recommendations)).

**Three spaces, two of which need gravity.** Local space uses the pad's own axes and no accelerometer. World space and player space both take the estimated gravity vector to tell a turn about the vertical from one about the pad's own axis; player space forgives an error in it of about 45° (a relax factor of 1.41; JoyShockMapper uses 2, about 60°), and world space shows any error as a cosine and as leakage between axes ([GyroWiki, "Player Space Gyro"](http://gyrowiki.jibbsmart.com/blog:player-space-gyro-and-alternatives-explained)). Player space is the default the library most games would copy recommends ([GamepadMotionHelpers](https://github.com/JibbSmart/GamepadMotionHelpers)).

**Gravity is estimated, not read.** The estimate is turned by the gyro and pulled toward the accelerometer, at a rate of about 1 a second when the pad is still, 0.1 when shaken, and capped near a tenth of the turning rate ([GyroWiki, "Finding Gravity with Sensor Fusion"](http://gyrowiki.jibbsmart.com/blog:finding-gravity-with-sensor-fusion)). "The accelerometer alone cannot reliably tell you the direction of gravity."

**Small rates are already treated specially.** Smoothing below a threshold and "tightening", a soft scaling down of rates under about 5°/s, are the community's answer to a hand's shake; a hard cutoff is advised against, and the way to say "I am not aiming now" is a button that turns the gyro off or on while held (JoyShockMapper's `GYRO_ON`/`GYRO_OFF`). An acceleration curve makes sensitivity grow with speed, so it is not linear in the rate.

**Bias is the game's to remove, and it assumes one pad.** The kernel's driver applies the pad's calibration for scale and subtracts no bias (a change of 2023), so whoever reads the sensor node calibrates drift itself. GamepadMotionHelpers does it two ways: when gyro and accelerometer are both still for a time, the mean is the bias; and by comparing the gyro with the turning the accelerometer's gravity implies, taking the difference for bias when the turning is steady. An uncorrected bias of 0.5°/s at a sensitivity of 2 is 60° of drift a minute.

**What a DualSense gives.** Through the kernel, a second input device, "Motion Sensors", with the accelerometer on `ABS_X/Y/Z` at 8192 to a g, the gyro on `ABS_RX/RY/RZ` at 1024 to a degree a second, and a timestamp in microseconds as `MSC_TIMESTAMP`. SDL pairs it with the pad by its `uniq` string and hands a game the rate in radians a second and acceleration in m/s² with gravity in it: readings, and no pose. Steam's own API adds a fused quaternion. Reports come at 250 Hz on USB and, by SDL's comments, 1000 Hz on Bluetooth.

**Aim assist goes when gyro is on.** Fortnite and The Finals say so of themselves. One player on gyro and the other on the stick loses the stick its aim assist in such games.

**Not found.** Measured noise or bias for these pads' sensors; a healthy hand's tremor in degrees a second from a primary source (reasoned to be 2 to 6°/s at its peaks, at 8 to 12 Hz, which is the band fine aiming is in); any game saying whether it reads rates or a platform's pose.

**What follows for two pads (inference from the above).**

- **Timestamps come first.** A virtual pad must make its own clock. Passing on each source pad's timestamps makes the clock jump on a change of source, and SDL turns a jump backward into a very long step, which every integrator downstream then takes.
- **Bias must be removed here, for each pad, before anything is mixed.** A game that has learned one pad's bias drifts on the other's; a sum carries both biases; and the game's own calibrator, fed two pads, either refuses to calibrate or learns a mixture. Each pad's stillness is detectable from its own two sensors.
- **A rate can't tell a held pad from an aiming one.** A pad lying on a table (under 0.3°/s, its accelerometer constant) is cleanly told from a held one. A held and idle hand is not told from one aiming finely: both are a few degrees a second. So the deadzone that makes priority work for a stick does not carry over to the gyro as it stands: a threshold high enough to pass over tremor drops fine aim, the lost start of each movement makes a turn come up short, and near the threshold the two pads trade places at the tremor's own rate.
- **A hard switch between pads is otherwise benign** in local space, like handing over a mouse: the camera stays where it was left.
- **A sum is exact for a rate** and lets either aim, and works well when the idle pad lies flat; held, the idle hand's tremor goes straight to the camera. The two pads' axes agree only if they are held alike.
- **An average halves the sensitivity** of whoever moves alone, which breaks the learned measure of a turn, and blended only while both move it changes gain by half at each crossing.
- **Gyro from one pad and accelerometer from another is the worst of the mixtures.** Local space doesn't notice. Player space is unaffected while the two pads' poses differ by less than about 45°, weakens past that and reverses past 90°. The gravity estimate is turned by one pad and pulled toward the other. And calibration by fusion takes one player's steady aiming, unseen by the other pad's accelerometer, for bias, and corrects it away. This weighs against the first position's "the primary's accelerometer always".
- **JoyShockMapper's way with two Joy-Cons** is to let each move the mouse by itself, each after its own calibration and curve: a sum of results, not of signals.

### The accelerometer: tilt, pose and shakes

**Steering by tilt is done three ways, and each takes a mixed signal differently.**

- *From gravity alone.* The angle is `roll = atan2(a.x, a.y)` of the accelerometer's reading, sometimes through a low-pass of about a second ([Unity's sample](https://docs.unity3d.com/2019.4/Documentation/Manual/MobileInput.html)). It has no memory: nothing to drift, nothing to recover. The Wii's games before its gyro could do nothing else, and a Dolphin bug shows them reading the raw vector, linear acceleration and all ([Dolphin 11776](https://bugs.dolphin-emu.org/issues/11776)).
- *From a pose the platform fuses.* The Switch and the PlayStation hand a game an orientation beside the readings; Steam's API hands a quaternion. Emulators that stand in for those platforms fuse it themselves, and their code is where the constants can be read.
- *From the gyro integrated.* Steam Input's own "gyro to joystick deflection" does this, and users report it losing its center, as an angle with nothing to anchor it would.

**How a fused pose recovers** after its gyro was fed turning its accelerometer never saw: a Mahony filter (Cemu, and with other gains the Switch emulators) pulls back at `Kp × sin(error)`, a time constant of 2 to 3.3 s; Madgwick's slews at a constant 11.5°/s; Dolphin's in about a quarter second; GamepadMotionHelpers closes a large error fast and the last 3° slowly. In all of them only pitch and roll come back, smoothly and with no jump; an error in heading is never corrected. One library ignores an accelerometer that disagrees by more than 10° for about 5 s before it recovers at all ([x-io Fusion](https://github.com/xioTechnologies/Fusion)).

**Some consumers check that gravity is 1 g long.** The Switch emulators' filter corrects drift only between 0.75 and 1.25 g, and takes a pad for still, to learn its bias, only between 0.9 and 1.1 g. GamepadMotionHelpers uses the direction alone and counts a change of length as shaking.

**Shakes are found in what is not gravity:** a magnitude over about 1.3 g for a quarter second ([Seismic](https://github.com/square/seismic)), or acceleration with gravity taken out over a threshold. A vector that is always exactly 1 g can never shake.

**Not found.** Which of the three ways any named racing game steers by; Sony's and Nintendo's own documents (what is above is from homebrew headers and emulators); how Steam fuses its quaternion; any report of a game misbehaving when gravity jumped. The consequences below rest on libraries' and emulators' code, not on games seen to do it.

**What follows for two pads (inference).**

- **The primary's gravity with the other's rate** leaves a gravity-only game steering by the primary alone, as if the assist weren't there; makes a fused pose follow the assist and then drift back over seconds, with the heading left off for good; and has a calibrator by fusion learn the assist's steady turning as bias.
- **Both switched together** steps a gravity-only game's steering to the other pad's tilt, which is what was asked; a fused pose settles for seconds each way; and a calibrator by fusion can take the swing for a bias of tens of degrees a second, a spin of the camera. The most damaging case where that calibrator is on.
- **Made up, as a rigid body turned by the merged rate,** is perfectly consistent and no filter can notice it. But nothing ties it to the real down: the gyros' bias walks the steering's center away, and whatever the assist turned stays turned after they let go, so the primary must hold their pad askew to drive straight. And it can't shake. Tying it back to the pads' measured gravity with a gain makes it a complementary filter, and then the assist's part fades in a time of one over the gain: a nudge, not a held angle.
- **The two vectors averaged** points along the bisector, which for a roll in one plane is exactly the mean of the two roll angles: a gravity-only game gets a true blend of the two steerings, the best of the options for such a game. Its length is `cos(half the angle between)` of a g: 0.97 at 30° apart, 0.90 at 52°, 0.75 at 83°, nothing at opposite. So it wants making 1 g long again, and guarding where the two pads are nearly opposed.
- **Blending tilt angles and making a gravity vector of the result** is sound, and for pads near level it is the same as blending the unit vectors, which has no singularity at a pad held on end and no wrap at 180°, so the vectors are the better thing to blend. What it loses: linear acceleration, unless carried apart; exact agreement with the gyro, which holds only near level; and it mixes two people's resting poses, since nobody holds a pad level. Blending each one's departure from their own rest, and adding the result to the active player's rest, avoids the last.
- **The signal a game is least likely to notice,** by this survey: gravity's direction as a weighted mean of the pads' directions, made 1 g long; linear acceleration carried apart if shakes matter; and any change of weights ramped, a step in them being a jump of gravity in small.

### What others do

**Nobody blends two motion sensors.** Every system found picks one, or shows both side by side for the game to choose.

- **PlayStation's assist controller.** Sony's accessibility page: "Some of the DualSense features, such as motion sensor, haptic feedback and adaptive triggers, will be automatically turned off when the controller is being used as an assist controller." So motion is the main controller's, the primary player's, alone. The support page lists haptics, adaptive triggers, the microphone and custom buttons as not available on the assist controller, and says nothing of the touch pad, the speaker, the light bar, or how two inputs at once combine ([support](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/support/hardware/second-controller-assistance/), [accessibility](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/accessibility/)). An Access controller, which has neither sensor nor touch pad, joined with a DualSense takes its "motion sensor, and touch pad swipe" from the DualSense.
- **Xbox's Controller Assist** links two controllers "as if they were a single controller"; its pads have no motion sensor or touchpad. How analog values combine isn't in what could be read of Microsoft's page; a paper of 2026 describes it as summed, two opposite steerings going straight.
- **Two Joy-Cons as one pad**, the nearest thing to two sensors merged: Nintendo's system gives a game both, apart; games mostly ignore the left; SDL and Steam take the right and SDL also offers each by name; JoyShockMapper defaults to ignoring the left and offers ignore-left, ignore-right, ignore-both and use-both.
- **Tools that group controllers.** reWASD gives its virtual gyro to the first controller of a group. Cemu takes motion from the first attached controller marked to use it, binds buttons controller by controller, and rumbles them all. Emulators generally let the device that gives motion be chosen apart from the one that gives buttons.
- **InputPlumber** makes a composite of several source devices, each with a group (gamepad, IMU, touchscreen and so on) and its own included and excluded events, a source that is blocked, or one kept for output only. Motion has a place of origin in its vocabulary, `Gyroscope:Left`, `:Right` or `:Center`, and touchpads likewise. Its virtual DualSense carries one stream of motion and one touchpad of two contacts, and advances the sensor timestamp itself because SDL takes differences of it.

**How people share a controller.** Interviews with fourteen people who do ([Ahmetovic et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02132v1)): keeping each one's controls strictly apart is more usual than overlapping them; the camera is the control most often handed to the helper; roles are mostly agreed before play, and some hand over everything for a hard part; and not knowing who has which control "can lead to confusion and frustration". A study of shared control ([Mascetti et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11218v1)) tells combining two commands from selecting one of them, and asks that a system show, as it happens, what the helper is doing.

**Tremor, measured.** Essential tremor at the hand, by gyroscope: 0.5°/s RMS where mild, 15.2°/s where severe, at about 6 Hz ([Technology and Health Care, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7369080)). Physiological tremor is at 6 to 12 Hz, a Parkinsonian rest tremor at 4 to 6. No tool was found that offers a filter as one for tremor; what there is are a speed below which output stops, a speed below which it is reduced or smoothed, and the 1€ filter, a low-pass whose cutoff rises with speed, which Steam and DS4Windows both use.

**A vocabulary of settings**, as it recurs across Steam Input, JoyShockMapper, DS4Windows, reWASD, InputPlumber and the emulators:

- *Source.* Which device gives motion; which of several sensors (left, right, both, neither); the first that can; motion off on a second device; which touchpad, and several onto one; a source blocked or kept for output alone.
- *Engagement.* Always on; on while a button is held, off while held, toggled; by a button, a touch of a stick or a pad, a grip; released to move the hands back ("ratcheting"); a command to recenter.
- *Space and axes.* Yaw, roll or a blend of them for the horizontal; local, player or world space; inversion; a scale for each axis; a region of a touchpad.
- *Filtering.* A speed below which nothing passes; a speed below which it is reduced or smoothed; the 1€ filter; calibration when still.
- *Conversion.* Passed through as it is, to a virtual pad that has sensors; or turned into a mouse, a stick's deflection by rate (a camera) or by angle (a tilt), buttons or swipes.

**Not found.** Microsoft's own account of how two controllers' values combine; Sony's on the touch pad, speaker and light bar under assist; how JoyShockMapper's use-both combines; anything from the accessibility organizations on a helper and motion controls.

## Where the research leaves the first position

Talked through with the maintainer on 2026-11-23. What was decided is under Decided after the research, and stands over this where the two differ: bias, above all, is not measured here.

- **The gyro's rate by a deadzone doesn't stand as written.** A rate can't tell a held hand from one aiming finely, and for a player with a tremor, whom this is for, the idle rate is higher still. What the tools use is engagement by a control: the assist's turning counts while they hold a button or touch something. That is the modes' promise kept by other means, an intervention begun and ended by the assist's own act, and it is what everyone who has built gyro aiming advises.
- **"The primary's accelerometer always" doesn't stand either.** A pad's two sensors want keeping together, or the game's own estimate of gravity and its calibration are misled. The candidates are now: the whole pair from whoever supplies motion at the moment, with the change ramped; or gravity's direction as a weighted mean of the two pads', made 1 g long, which is what gives a game that steers by gravity a true blend.
- **Two things are needed whichever way it goes:** each pad's bias taken out here, by its own stillness, before anything is mixed; and the virtual pad's own clock for its sensor timestamps.
- **The settled practice elsewhere is one source, selected.** Sony's own feature is motion from the primary's pad and none from the assist's. That is the least surprising default for every mode, with the assist's part something a profile opts into.
- **Who has the motion wants showing.** Both studies ask for it, and the tray and the window already say who has control.
- **A profile would need new words for it,** which is the maintainer's thought of widening presets: at least where motion comes from, what engages the assist's, and how gravity is made. The vocabulary above is what such words have been elsewhere. Converting motion into a stick or a mouse is a remapper's work and stays out.

## Decided after the research

By the maintainer, 2026-11-23. These stand over the first position where the two differ.

- **With nothing said in a profile, motion and the touchpad's position are the primary's pad's,** as PlayStation's own assist feature has it: gyroscope, accelerometer and touchpad together from the one pad in priority and average, and from the pad that has control in toggle. The assist's sensors are left out unless a profile asks for them. A game then sees one pad's two sensors, which agree.
- **An assist engages their gyroscope by touching a control the profile names,** not by turning past a rate. While it is held, the assist's motion stands in for the primary's.
- **The order of work:** the plan's decisions now; a spike once the review's fixes have settled; the build after 0.5.0. The build began before 0.5.0 instead, by the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-23 (see Deviations).

Then, the same day, the open questions one at a time, once the spike had been run and the steps written:

- **Bias is not measured.** CtrlAssist has no need to know a pad's bias. The spike found the pad's stored bias wrong, a drift of about 25° a minute, but that is as true of the real pad read directly, and Steam and games already cope with it. The aim is parity with the real pad: the pad's readings carried with the pad's own stored calibration applied, and written again under the virtual pad's neutral calibration. Nothing is measured at rest, so there is no length of stillness and no duration. What parity means for each program that reads the pad, and which one the build matches, is in the step that reads the raw report. Measuring at rest is an idea, [gyro-bias-at-rest.md](../ideas/gyro-bias-at-rest.md).
- **While the assist's gyroscope is engaged, the assist's whole pair stands in:** gyroscope and accelerometer both from the assist's pad, switched at once as the button is pressed and let go, with no ramp. A game then always sees one pad's two sensors, which agree; the research found gyroscope from one pad and gravity from another the worst of the mixtures.
- **In average, nothing of motion or touch is blended.** Motion and the touchpad's contacts are the primary's pad's, as in priority; the touchpad's click is a button and follows average's rule for buttons; an assist still joins by the engaging button. Two fingers' places don't average into a third that means anything, and a blend of rates halves the gain of whoever moves alone.
- **Steering by tilt has no control of its own outside toggle,** for now. An engaged assist's pair carries their tilt with their turning, so an assist steers by tilt while they hold the button.
- **The engaging button is kept from the game** on the assist's input, as a switch's button is: the assist's button does that and nothing else, and the primary's same button is untouched.
- **Engaging is refused in toggle.** A toggle profile that names an engaging button is rejected when it is checked, saying that motion follows the pad that has control: everything comes from that pad, which is toggle's promise, and an assist who wants the motion takes control.
- **The virtual pad's own touchpad is left to the desktop:** it moves the pointer as a real DualSense's does, and Steam or a game takes it when they use it. A desktop or Steam setting that turns a pad's touchpad off turns the virtual one's off too. The hidden pad's touchpad is kept from the compositor whatever the setup, as built.
- **The vocabulary stays that small:** one key in a preset, the assist's engaging button, and `gyro = { button, input }` on a mux in the full form. Where motion comes from is otherwise the mode's and is no key, and there is no new kind of node. The names (`assist-gyro` for the preset's key) stay proposals until the step that builds them.
- **The virtual pad's calibration is neutral, and CtrlAssist corrects each pad's counts as SDL does.** A real DualSense sends counts and a stored calibration report, and its two readers do their own arithmetic with it: SDL, which games and Steam read through, takes the stored gyroscope bias off; the kernel's driver, for its motion node, does not. The other way considered was to give the virtual pad the primary's own report and pass its counts as they are, converting another pad's counts to come right under it. The neutral report was chosen for having one path for every pad and nothing that depends on which pad is the primary or when it connects; what it gives up is small, the stored bias being itself wrong by as much, as the spike measured. The whole comparison, kept in case this is looked at again:

  | | The primary's report | A neutral report, SDL's way (chosen) |
  |---|---|---|
  | SDL, the primary's motion | Exact | Exact |
  | SDL, another pad's motion standing in | Exact, its counts converted | Exact |
  | The kernel's motion node, the primary's motion | Exact | Off by the pad's stored bias, under 1 °/s on the spike's pad |
  | The kernel's motion node, another pad standing in | Off by the difference between the two pads' stored biases | Off by that pad's stored bias |
  | Toggle | Needs a rule for whose report it is; the other pad always stands in | Nothing of its own |
  | The primary not connected as the setup starts | No report to give; a fallback, so what a reader sees depends on the order of starting | Nothing of its own |
  | The primary swapped or replaced as the setup runs | The report is stale, readers reading it once | Nothing of its own |
  | The source is a DualShock 4 | Its report is laid out otherwise and must be made a DualSense's | The path any pad takes |
  | The architecture as built | Replaces its neutral report | As it is |
  | To be tested | Two paths, the primary and a stand-in, in each case above | One path |

  Readers of the kernel's motion node are not only imagined: some emulators and motion tools read it and not SDL. They would see a drift a little other than the real pad's, and each has its own calibration or recentering.

What is still open is under Open questions.

## What the spike found

`spikes/motion` carried one real DualSense on USB, hidden by the installed hiding service, to a virtual DualSense: buttons and sticks from its event node, both sensors, their timestamp, the touchpad's contacts and click from its raw report. Run on 2026-11-23, on Linux 7.3. A game was not tried; the numbers are from the spike's own record of every report, about 350,000 of them.

- **A virtual DualSense's sensors are taken for a pad's.** The kernel's driver made the motion, touchpad and headset nodes and its motion node followed the pad, seen in jstest-gtk; Steam's controller test showed the sensors and its motion calibration ran to its end as with a real pad.
- **The pad's reports come every 4.00 ms on USB,** by its own clock and as they arrive, the two differing by 0.13 ms from report to report. Over turns of 22 s and 47 s the angle summed by the pad's clock and by the arrival time agreed to 0.01°. Which clock the virtual pad stamps with made no difference that could be measured here, with the machine idle; the longest gap between arrivals in the whole run was 9.5 ms.
- **The pad's own calibration of scale is close enough to the usual pad's not to matter.** The three axes' scales differ from the nominal by under 0.4 %. The pad was spun flat on a desk against one straight edge, two full turns one way and one back: with the bias measured at rest taken off, the sums were 716.5° of 720° and 351.4° of 360°, within what turning by hand allows.
- **The bias the pad was calibrated with is wrong, and is the whole of the error.** At rest this pad reads 4.5, 6.7 and 0.2 counts; its calibration says 4, 15 and -5. Taking the stored bias off, as SDL does, drifts 26° a minute one way; taking none off, as the kernel's driver does, drifts 24° a minute the other. The bias measured over 20 s at rest left under 1° a minute. Steam's calibration does this measuring for itself; a game that trusts the pad does not. The research's point read this as a requirement, that whoever combines pads measures bias at rest, per pad; the maintainer decided otherwise (Decided after the research): the pad read directly drifts as much, so the virtual pad carries the pad's readings with its stored calibration applied, and what it says of its own calibration is neutral.
- **The compositor holds both touchpads.** With the spike running, KWin's own list of input devices, read over the session bus, had two enabled touchpads for the one pad: the hidden pad's, its node the hiding service's group's alone, and the virtual pad's. A hidden pad's touchpad was seen to drive the pointer, and the virtual pad's is a touchpad like any other, so a finger on the pad moves the pointer by both. How fast the pointer went by eye showed nothing either way: a DualSense's touchpad is faster than a DualShock 4's with no setup at all. A setup that gives its virtual pad a touchpad must keep the hidden pad's from the compositor, by the rule telling libinput to ignore it; whether the virtual pad's own should drive the pointer is the question that was already open.
- **The neutral calibration is not one count to a unit for the gyroscope.** The kernel's driver and SDL both read the virtual pad's calibration report as 1080 °/s over 17678 counts, about 16.4 counts to the degree a second. A rate taken from a pad's motion node, at 1024 to the degree a second, and written as counts would turn about 62 times too fast; the accelerometer's 8192 to the g is the same on both sides. So the architecture's line that the virtual DualSense is fed values the pad's driver already calibrated holds for the accelerometer alone: a rate is converted to the neutral report's counts. The line now says so. Read in `drivers/hid/hid-playstation.c` and SDL's `SDL_hidapi_ps5.c`, as the spike's README says.
- **A hidden DualSense's touchpad click is not read.** The kernel's driver reports it as `BTN_LEFT` on the touchpad's node and not on the pad's, which is why the spike takes it from the raw report. The device crate reads a pad of that driver by the kernel's layout from its event node alone, never through the controller database (which names a `touchpad` button, at button 20), and the virtual DualSense's input report never sets the click's bit. So the first position's "its click is already a button" was wrong for these pads: while a setup hides one, the click is lost with the touchpad.

Not found out: whether a game that reads the pad through SDL, or through Proton, aims and steers as with the real pad; anything on Bluetooth; anything with the machine busy, where the two clocks may part; a DualShock 4, whose report the spike doesn't read.

The steps take four requirements from it. The virtual pad presents the neutral calibration, each pad's readings carried with its own stored calibration applied and the rate converted to the neutral report's counts. The virtual pad's sensor time is a clock of its own, never a source pad's passed on. Motion and touch come from the pad's raw report, through the hiding service's `raw of`, which also gives the touchpad's click. A hidden pad's touchpad is kept from the compositor by the hiding rule.

## Steps

Each is one reviewed change on a branch of its own, and leaves the tree working: verified, beside what it names, by the suites that make no pads, by the whole suite while Steam is closed, and by the VM checks. Each step corrects the architecture where it makes it untrue, in the same change; the last migrates the plan.

1. **The hidden pad's touchpad kept from the compositor.** *Done,* before 0.5.0, as State says; where it departs from what is written here is under Deviations: the property on every event node the rule hides, a touchpad hidden late sent a synthetic remove and add, and the check the hiding service's. The hiding rule sets `LIBINPUT_IGNORE_DEVICE=1` on a hidden pad's touchpad node, where it hides the node, so that libinput, and the compositor through it, leaves the node alone while the pad is hidden. A hide re-binds the pad's driver, so the node is made anew with the property, which libinput reads as it adds a device; showing the pad again re-binds it again, and the node comes back without it. It applies to every hidden pad's touchpad, whatever the setup's virtual pads, which the rule can't know. It depends on nothing else here, and stops a fault seen today, a hidden pad's touchpad moving the pointer.
   - *Where:* `packaging/udev/72-ctrlassist-hide.rules`, a line for a node with `ID_INPUT_TOUCHPAD` under its `ctrlassist_hide` label; the architecture's section on the rule.
   - *Verified:* the daemon's VM check, which makes a fake DualSense through uhid that the kernel's driver gives a touchpad node, and has the service hide it: the touchpad's event node carries the property (`udevadm info`) and libinput's tools in the VM don't list it; shown again, the property is gone and it is listed. On hardware: KWin's list of input devices over the session bus, read as the spike read it, has no touchpad for a hidden DualSense.
   - *To find out in it:* a pad that reconnects while hidden has its touchpad's node hidden late, by the change event its joystick node's event has udev send, after libinput may have added it. Whether libinput lets go of a device that a change event marks to be ignored is to be seen in the VM across a reconnect (`libinput debug-events`). If it doesn't, the gap is written down as a concern beside the one the architecture already gives for those nodes, not closed with a wait.
   - *Leaves out:* the touchpad's mouse node, which libinput doesn't read.
2. **Motion and touch in the pad state, routed by default.** *Done,* as State says; where it departs from what is written here is under Deviations. The core's pad state gains, apart from its 64 buttons and 16 axes, two fixed-size blocks. Motion: the gyroscope's rate on three axes in degrees a second, the accelerometer on three in g, and, so that a virtual pad can tell a fresh reading and keep a clock of its own, a count of the source pad's readings and the time since its last one by its own clock (proposed). The touchpad: two contacts, each down or not, with a place across and down from 0 to 1. At rest, in `NEUTRAL`, motion is a pad lying flat and still, as the virtual DualSense reports now, and no finger is down. The click is a button, at the position the controller database already gives `touchpad` (button 20), named in `gamepad`. Each value is set through a check, as an axis is: one that isn't a number reads as rest and a place is held to 0 to 1, so nothing a report says can make a step panic. They are not controls, and no group rule applies to them: a mux takes both blocks whole from one input, the first in priority and average (a preset's primary) and the one selected in toggle; a demux sends them with the rest, to the output selected and the others at rest in unicast, to every output in multicast. That is the default routing the maintainer decided. Nothing writes motion yet, so no running setup changes.
   - *Where:* `crates/ctrlassist-core/src/{control.rs,mux.rs,demux.rs}`. `graph.rs` emits no change for them: a virtual pad is written its whole state.
   - *Verified:* unit tests of the mux in each mode (the primary's motion and contacts whatever the assist does, in priority and average; the selected pad's in toggle, following a switch and a pad that goes) and of the demux; the scenario tests; the allocation test with motion in the step; the property test that no value of a block panics a step or reaches a sink as other than a number.
   - *Leaves out:* any reader or writer of motion; the assist's part. A switch in toggle moves a finger held down from one place to another in one report, as it steps a stick; whether a game that reads the touchpad takes that for a swipe is to be seen on hardware, in toggle, once pads' touchpads are read.
3. **The virtual DualSense writes sensors, clock, contacts and click.** The emulator's input report takes the pad state's motion and touchpad: the rate as counts under the neutral calibration it already gives (1080 °/s over 17678 counts), gravity at 8192 counts to the g, each held to what an `i16` holds; both contacts packed as the pad packs them, and the click in the third button byte. The calibration report stays neutral: what reaches the emulator has the source pad's own stored calibration applied already (the raw report's step says which reading of it). The sensor time is the virtual pad's own clock. A fresh reading moves it by what is left of the source pad's own interval since its last fresh reading; a report with none, a button's or a beat, by the time since the last report; and no report leaves it where it was, so it neither jumps on a change of source nor stands still. Every fresh reading is written, though nothing else in the report changed, as a real pad reports each one.
   - *Where:* `crates/ctrlassist-device/src/dualsense.rs` (`input_report`, `write`, `beat`, the clock). The test crate's fake DualSense (`crates/ctrlassist-test/src/hid.rs`, the fixture's `fake-dualsense`) is made from this emulator, and is given motion and contacts to report, for the checks after.
   - *Verified:* unit tests: a state's motion and contacts come back from the report (the spike's tests, moved); a count written, read by the neutral calibration as the kernel's driver and as SDL read it, is the rate written to within a count; the clock follows a source's intervals, doesn't jump at a change of source, and never stands still. The device crate's VM check: a virtual DualSense written a known rate and gravity shows them on its motion node through the kernel's driver, at 1024 to the °/s and 8192 to the g, and its touchpad node shows the contacts and the click.
   - *Gated by:* a game through SDL and Proton; what SDL does with a sensor time that hasn't moved.
   - *Leaves out:* any pad's motion: the virtual pad reports flat and still, on its own clock, until the next step.
4. **A DualSense's raw report read on the input thread.** A DualSense given its raw node (hidden by the service: `give_raw`) has that node read as well as written, on a duplicate of its descriptor, with no second request of the service. The input thread listens to it beside the pad's event node, under a token of its own. On its readiness it reads every report waiting, without blocking, into a buffer the pad holds; a USB report (0x01, 64 bytes) or a Bluetooth one (0x31, 78 bytes, the same body a byte later) gives the six sensor counts, the pad's sensor time, both contacts and the click, and any other report is passed over. Counts become rate and gravity by the pad's calibration report (0x05), read as the pad is given its raw node, on the control side, since the request waits on the pad: applied as SDL applies it, with the stored bias off, and a report SDL would doubt replaced by nominal scales, so no scale reaches the thread as zero or infinite. Nothing is measured: the bias the pad stores is the bias taken off. The event node's frames step the graph as now, and each raw report steps it with the new motion and touch; with both ready, the event node is read first, as the kernel's driver puts a report's frame on the event node before the raw node has the report. Nothing here allocates, logs or waits: a read that would block ends the turn; the node gone (`ENODEV`) is the pad gone, as for its event node; any other error leaves the pad's motion and touch at rest for the rest of its time in the setup (proposed). With this the daemon does what the spike did, and `spikes/motion` is deleted.
   - *What parity means, reader by reader.* Parity is that a program reading the virtual pad sees what it would reading the pad itself. Two readers of a DualSense apply its calibration report differently. SDL, reading the raw node, takes the stored gyroscope bias off and applies the scales. The kernel's driver, whose motion node is what reads the pad through evdev (SDL where it can't open the raw node, and anything else on the event nodes), applies the scales and takes no gyroscope bias off. For the accelerometer the two agree. On the virtual pad, though, both apply the same neutral report, whose bias is nothing, so both see the numbers the build writes, and those can match one reader of the real pad, not both. The build matches SDL's: the stored bias off, which is the decision's "the pad's own stored calibration applied", and the reading SDL hands a game that opens the pad by its raw node. A program reading the virtual pad's motion node then sees the stored bias taken off where, on the pad's own node, it wouldn't: on the spike's pad 4, 15 and -5 counts, under a degree a second, a drift of 26° a minute one way where the pad's own node drifts 24° the other. Steam, which measures at rest for itself, corrects either.
   - *Decided for SDL's reader (see Decided after the research); the other way, for the record.* Both readers could have parity for one pad, by giving the virtual pad's calibration report that pad's stored gyroscope bias in place of nothing and writing its counts with no bias off: SDL would take the bias off and the kernel's driver wouldn't, each as with the pad. But the report is read once, as the virtual pad is bound and as SDL opens it, and a setup's virtual pad carries more than one pad's motion: an engaged assist's, the pad selected in toggle, a pad that takes the place of one gone. Writing another pad's counts so that SDL still sees that pad's stored calibration applied leaves the kernel's reader off by the difference of the two pads' stored biases. So it is SDL's parity always, as written, or both readers' while the primary's motion is carried and SDL's alone while another pad's is; the second also departs from the architecture's neutral report and needs the primary's calibration before the virtual pad is made. The steps are written for the first.
   - *Where:* `crates/ctrlassist-device/src/{pad.rs,thread.rs,hidraw.rs}`, and a module of its own for the report, its calibration and the clock, from `spikes/motion/src/report.rs`; `crates/ctrlassist-daemon/src/setups.rs` (`give_raw`).
   - *Verified:* the spike's unit tests of the report and the calibration, moved. The daemon's VM check: the fake DualSense, hidden by the service, reports a known rate, gravity and a contact, and the setup's virtual DualSense shows them on its motion and touchpad nodes: in priority from the primary and never the assist, in toggle from the pad selected, following a switch. `just bench` with a fake DualSense reporting at 250 and 1000 reports a second, idle and with every CPU busy, against the architecture's table. On hardware, the spike's checks through the daemon: Steam's controller test shows the sensors and its motion calibration runs to its end, and turns on a desk's edge sum as the spike's did, drifting as the pad read through SDL does and no more.
   - *Gated by:* Bluetooth; load.
   - *Leaves out:* measuring a pad's bias at rest ([the idea](../ideas/gyro-bias-at-rest.md)); a DualShock 4; a DualSense Edge, which the device crate's writer doesn't take either; a pad with no raw node (no hiding service, `hide = "grab"` or `"none"`), whose motion rests, as every pad's does today. Whether a pad whose motion no virtual pad carries need be stepped on each raw report is left to the bench to say.
5. **The DualShock 4's report.** A DualShock 4 given its raw node (the device crate's writer takes one already) is read for motion as a DualSense is, by its own layout: its input report over USB and over Bluetooth, a sensor clock narrower than the DualSense's and in other units, its touch data laid out otherwise, its touchpad of another size, and its calibration under another report number and in another order by transport. All of that is to be read in the two sources named under Still to find out before the step is written; none of it has been tried here. Its click goes to the same button, its contacts to the same places, scaled from its own touchpad's size.
   - *Where:* the device crate's motion module.
   - *Verified:* unit tests from reports laid out as the sources say. On hardware, the maintainer's DualShock 4 on Bluetooth, and on USB if it can be, behind a virtual DualSense: Steam's controller test, turns on a desk's edge, drift at rest no more than the pad's read through SDL. The test crate has no fake DualShock 4, which would need the pad's report descriptor, which the device crate doesn't carry; whether to add one for the VM is the step's to decide.
   - *Gated by:* a DualShock 4.
   - *Leaves out:* a DualShock 4 as a virtual pad's identity.
6. **The assist engages their gyroscope.** A mux may name a button, and whose it is, that hands motion over while it is held: while that input holds it, its motion, both sensors, stands in for the motion the mode takes, in priority and in average alike; let go, the mode's pad has it again at its next report. Building a graph checks it as it checks a switch (a button, on one of the node's inputs), and refuses one that is the node's switch too.    - *Where:* `crates/ctrlassist-core/src/{mux.rs,graph.rs}`; `crates/ctrlassist-protocol/src/{profile.rs,form.rs}`; the book's profiles page.

## Deviations

- **The hidden pad's touchpad landed before 0.5.0,** where the plan put the whole build after it: it stops a fault seen today, and depends on nothing else here.
- **The rest of the build began before 0.5.0 too,** by the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-23, where the plan put it after.
- **A mux takes motion and the contacts from the input selected wherever it selects,** not by its mode: the core's mux keeps its rules and not the mode they came from, and a profile's full form may give a priority or average mux a switched rule, or a toggle mux none. A mux that selects (any group switched) takes the blocks from the input selected, and one that doesn't from its first, which is the step's rule for the three modes as the presets make them, and is where such a mux's feedback goes too.
- **A reading with any value that isn't a finite number lies still as a whole,** the rate and gravity both, keeping its count and interval: a gravity with one axis put at rest points nowhere real. An infinity is no number here, as for an axis. A place that isn't a finite number makes its contact lifted, at the corner; one that is is held to 0 to 1.
- **A lifted contact keeps its place,** as a DualSense's report keeps the place its finger left, so that the virtual pad can report a lifted finger as the pad does.
- **Beyond the core:** the controller database's `touchpad` names `gamepad::TOUCHPAD`, where it gave the same position by number (`crates/ctrlassist-device/src/database.rs`); a uinput virtual pad is boxed in the device crate's sink, as a DualSense's already was, the pad state it keeps having grown past clippy's bound on an enum's variants (`crates/ctrlassist-device/src/sink.rs`); and the architecture's core model says what the pad state now holds and how a mux and a demux route it.
- **The property is set on every event node the rule hides,** not on the touchpad's alone: a hidden pad is absent, and libinput, which passes over a joystick and motion sensors by itself, then doesn't even open them. The mouse node is left without it, libinput reading event nodes alone; nothing current reads a touchpad's mouse node, and `/dev/input/mice`, which merges them all, is no desktop's.
- **A touchpad hidden late is let go by libinput,** where the step said to write that gap down as a concern: libinput lets go of a node at its remove event and reads the property at its add, so the rule, hiding a touchpad's event node on a change event, has udev send it a remove and an add, the node itself staying as it is. No wait is involved. Hiding such a node at its own add instead, by its markers, was looked at and set aside: without the group-write bit, which only the joystick's event sets, nothing at that add says the device is a pad's, and a port's marker names whatever is plugged there, a touchpad of its own among them, and a serial number names every interface of its device, where a pad shown again is re-bound at its own alone.
- **Verified in the hiding service's check, not the daemon's:** the step is the rule's alone, and the hider check has a compositor at the seat to hold the node.
