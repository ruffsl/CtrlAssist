# Workspace review before 0.5.0

## Goal

The rewrite has reached rough feature parity with 0.4.0: the daemon, the tray and the settings window all work. Before 0.5.0 the whole workspace was reviewed, with two further questions alongside: what of the TisVlay template wasn't carried over, and whether the hiding service can do its work without root.

Ten read-only agents did the review on 2026-11-22, one per area: core and protocol; device; daemon; the hiding service's security (with an inventory of its privileged operations); SDK and CLI; tray; settings window; packaging, tooling and docs; an audit against the TisVlay template; and research on running the hiding service without root. Each finding was asked to state where it is, a scenario in which it fails, its severity, and whether it was verified or only suspected. None of them edited the repo, and none ran a test that creates fake pads.

This plan records every finding once, triaged by area, with stable ids so commits and agents can cite them. Where several agents found the same thing on their own, the finding says so, since that raises confidence. Severities and verification status are the agents' own.

## Decisions

Made by the maintainer on 2026-11-22.

- **The findings are recorded in this one triaged plan.** One list, by area, is what the parallel fix work below needs to divide and tick off; separate concern docs per finding would scatter it.
- **The hiding service's direction is decided by spikes first.** The first spike replaces the rules written at runtime with one static udev rule keyed on marker files, since that is the move that matters whether root stays or goes (see [The hiding service without root](#the-hiding-service-without-root)). Then a choice between dropping root (a dedicated user, group-owned hidden nodes, group-writable bind and unbind for a short driver list) and keeping a shrunk root. The two high-severity gaps, H-1 and H-2, are fixed in whichever shape results, so they aren't fixed twice.
- **Shipped builds unwind on panic.** The input thread's per-setup isolation then holds as the architecture promises: a setup whose step panics is torn down alone, and the other setups' virtual pads stay. The `dist` profile's `panic = "abort"` goes (R-1).
- **Fixes are made by area in parallel,** each area on its own branch and worktree, then merged. The areas touch mostly disjoint crates, so they can proceed without waiting on each other.
- **The oldest supported distribution may move up to Ubuntu 26.04 or newer** if that helps the hiding service. A spike result that needs a newer udev than the ones the research named (Debian 12's 252, Ubuntu 22.04's 249) is therefore acceptable.

## Findings

Each finding has a checkbox to tick when fixed. Severity and verification are as the reviewing agent gave them; "suspected" means the agent did not confirm it.

### Hiding service (security)

Paths are under `crates/ctrlassist-hider/src/` unless given in full. From the security review, except where noted.

- [ ] **H-1. Possession of a descriptor doesn't prove access: an O_PATH descriptor names any pad.** `pad.rs:184-192` (`identify`), used by Hide at `main.rs:86` and Raw at `pad.rs:232-235` / `main.rs:166`. `identify` only `fstat`s the descriptor and takes `st_rdev`: it never checks it wasn't opened O_PATH, makes no evdev ioctl on it, and `classify` (`pad.rs:118-154`) never compares the pad's seat (`ID_SEAT`) with the caller's. Any user can open `/dev/input/eventN` with O_PATH (needs only search permission on `/dev/input`) and pass it: on a multi-seat machine the seat1 user hides a seat0 pad, its driver is re-bound (cutting off seat0's game), and gets back a read-write descriptor that can inject events; Raw does the same for a DualSense's hidraw node (arbitrary output and feature reports). With H-3, a backgrounded user does this to the foreground user's pads on one seat. Fix: require an evdev ioctl (EVIOCGVERSION or EVIOCGID) to succeed on the received descriptor, or an F_GETFL access mode that isn't O_PATH; require the pad's `ID_SEAT` (default seat0) to equal the caller's seat. **Severity: high** on multi-seat machines and for a backgrounded user; low on a single seat with one user. **Verified** in the code; the O_PATH kernel behavior is from documentation, not tested.
- [ ] **H-2. A pad is found again by an identity the seat user can forge, not by the pad that was checked.** `pad.rs:199-218` (`find`), called through `nodes`/`family`/`rebind` from `hide.rs:139-140, 250-255, 280` and `pad.rs:223, 240, 249`. `find` returns the first event node whose vendor, product, uniq and serial match, without comparing bus, seat, syspath or the `uaccess` tag, and skipping `classify`'s exclusions. `hide()` re-binds the presented pad but records, opens and returns `find()`'s (`hide.rs:280, 321`); Raw checks the presented pad's driver (`pad.rs:233-234`) but opens `family(identity)`'s hidraw node. Scenario: the seat user makes a uhid device with another pad's vendor, product and address (uniq is world-readable in sysfs); it sits on BUS_VIRTUAL, so hid-generic binds it and the PlayStation driver's duplicate-address check never runs. Hiding the forged one, `find` returns the real pad (`/sys/devices/pci…` sorts before `/sys/devices/virtual/…`): the caller gets a read-write descriptor on it, RawOf gives its hidraw node, restore later re-binds its driver, the rule denies it, and a later Hide of it is refused as Busy (`main.rs:93-96`). Two identical pads with no uniq or serial collide the same way without forging anything. Reopen and restore's re-bind also use `find`, so they are affected too. Fix: record the HID or input syspath at hide time and act on that; re-identify only after the pad has actually been removed, then require bus and seat equality plus `classify`'s full checks; in Raw, open the hidraw node under the presented pad. **Severity: high** on multi-seat; medium-low on a single seat. **Verified** in the code; the uhid fields and enumeration order are from knowledge.
- [ ] **H-3. "Stays permitted" covers new requests, not just keeping what was hidden.** `main.rs:65-67, 241-251`. Once permitted, a connection may Hide, Raw and Reopen indefinitely; the architecture promises only that it "keeps what it has hidden". When user B switches in, A's daemon's connection can still hide B's pads and receive descriptors and hidraw nodes for them (O_PATH per H-1), or reopen pads it hid; another process of A can reach the connection by ptrace or `pidfd_getfd` where Yama allows. Fix: after the user leaves the foreground, allow only Restore (perhaps Reopen of existing tokens). **Severity: medium. Verified** in the code.
- [ ] **H-4. Any local user can hang the service by not reading replies.** `main.rs:46-50, 69`, `wire.rs:250-255`; accepted sockets are blocking (`main.rs:240`) and `send` passes only NOSIGNAL; the socket is 0666 (`packaging/systemd/ctrlassist-hider.socket:9`) and unpermitted clients are still answered. A client sends a few hundred requests and never reads: the send buffer fills and `sendmsg` blocks forever on the only thread, so no further hide or restore, and the daemon's close is never processed, leaving pads hidden until an admin restarts the service. Fix: send with MSG_DONTWAIT and drop the client on EAGAIN; don't reply to unpermitted clients. **Severity: medium. Verified** in the code; the SEQPACKET send-buffer blocking is from knowledge.
- [ ] **H-5. Received descriptors are closed on the service thread, so a FUSE file can block it.** `main.rs:59-71` (dropped even for unpermitted clients), `main.rs:103`, `pad.rs:185` (`fstat`). Any user mounts FUSE, opens a file and passes it: `close` sends FUSE_FLUSH and waits forever, and `fstat` waits on GETATTR the same way (a TCP socket with SO_LINGER and unsent data blocks `close` too). Same effect as H-4. Fix: close received descriptors on a helper thread; check the permission before acting on descriptors. **Severity: medium.** The code path is **verified**; the kernel behavior is **suspected**, not tested.
- [ ] **H-6. Running out of descriptors makes the service exit.** `main.rs:240` (`accept(listener)?`), connections kept without limit (`main.rs:246`). About 1020 idle connections reach the default soft LimitNOFILE of 1024; `accept` fails with EMFILE, `run` returns Err and the process exits. ExecStopPost's `reconcile` then shows every hidden pad to games mid-session, and with `StartLimitIntervalSec=0` (`ctrlassist-hider.service:8`) it repeats indefinitely. Fix: treat accept errors as non-fatal; cap connections per uid. **Severity: medium-low. Verified** in the code; the default limit is from knowledge.
- [ ] **H-7. The udev rule's uinput and uhid grant.** `packaging/udev/70-ctrlassist.rules:6-7`. When the session changes the ACL is revoked but open descriptors stay valid, so user A can keep a `/dev/uinput` descriptor, switch away, and type into B's session through a virtual keyboard. uhid also lets the seat user feed every HID driver's report parser, a recurring source of kernel CVEs. A known trade-off that Steam's rules share. Worth considering: the daemon receives uinput and uhid descriptors through the service rather than through uaccess. **Severity: medium. Suspected** (logind and kernel behavior from knowledge).
- [ ] **H-8. Hiding can be undone by other rules, and spills over to other pads.** `hide.rs:66`. The rule uses `MODE=`, `GROUP=` and `TAG-=`, not `MODE:=` / `GROUP:=`, so a later rule (a `99-*.rules` with `MODE="0666"` or `TAG+="uaccess"`) silently undoes hiding. A pad with no well-formed uniq or serial gets a model-only rule (`hide.rs:67-75, 82-85`), which denies every pad of that model on every seat at its next event. **Severity: low. Verified** in the code.
- [ ] **H-9. After 65,535 HID devices since boot, hiding stops cutting readers off.** `pad.rs:67-74`. The kernel's `%04X` instance numbers grow to five digits past 0xFFFF; `is_hid_name` then fails, `rebind` is None, and Hide falls back to retrigger only (`dislodged=false`), so Steam keeps the pad. The seat user can force this quickly with uhid. **Severity: low. Verified** in the code; the kernel naming is from knowledge.
- [ ] **H-10. The listening and client sockets leak into every `udevadm` child.** `activation.rs:21`, `main.rs:240`. fd 3 from systemd isn't set CLOEXEC, rustix 1.1.6's `accept` calls plain `accept(2)` without SOCK_CLOEXEC (`backend/linux_raw/net/syscalls.rs:133-137`), and the LISTEN_* variables aren't unset. **Severity: low**, hygiene. **Verified.**
- [ ] **H-11. The unit's hardening doesn't contain a compromised service.** `packaging/systemd/ctrlassist-hider.service:20-38`. The service's core power is writing udev rules, and a rule with `RUN+=` runs any program as unconfined root; `/sys` is writable (ProtectKernelTunables can't be set) and so is all of `/run` (`ReadWritePaths=/run`), which allows writing `/run/systemd/system/*.service`. So the bounding set doesn't stop escalation; the rule-text validation is the real boundary. Cheap additions still worthwhile: `DevicePolicy=closed` with `DeviceAllow=char-input rw` and `char-hidraw rw`; `ReadWritePaths` narrowed to `/run/ctrlassist` and `-/run/udev/rules.d`; `ProtectProc=invisible`, `ProcSubset=pid`, `PrivateIPC=yes`; `UMask=0077` (records and rules are world-readable, though they hold only addresses sysfs shows). The TisVlay audit independently found the unit lacking hardening TisVlay's daemon unit has: `UMask=0077`, `ProtectProc=invisible`, `ProcSubset=pid`, `SystemCallFilter=~@resources`, `RemoveIPC`, noting each needs checking against what the service does. The research's K1 design goes further (see below). **Severity: low** (security review); unclear (TisVlay audit). **Verified** in the code and units.
- [ ] **H-12. `--listen` sets the socket's mode by path after binding.** `main.rs:258-264`. `set_permissions` follows a symlink swapped in after `bind`; needs root running `--listen` in a world-writable directory, mitigated by `fs.protected_symlinks`. Fix: `fchmod` the socket, or set a umask before `bind`. **Severity: low. Verified.**
- [ ] **H-13. Truncated requests are accepted.** `wire.rs:271-283`. A request longer than 5 bytes is cut by the kernel and read as its first 5; MSG_TRUNC isn't checked. **Informational. Verified** by reading.
- [ ] **H-14. Tokens wrap at 2^32.** `main.rs:105`. A reused token could overwrite a live rule or record path, but it takes about 4 billion hides. **Informational**, not practical. **Verified** by reading.

### Core and protocol

Paths under `crates/ctrlassist-core/src/` or `crates/ctrlassist-protocol/src/` as given. The reviewer verified with throwaway probe binaries in the scratchpad.

- [x] **C-1. A small profile can hang the input thread through an exponential feedback walk.** `crates/ctrlassist-core/src/graph.rs:351-371` (`targets_of`). It recurses into every mux input without remembering visited nodes, so a node reached by k paths is walked k times; `Graph::build` caps neither node count nor depth and allows duplicate inputs. A chain of muxes each listing the previous node 8 times: a 20-node version is 1,887 bytes, passes `Profile::check`, is far under the daemon's 64 KiB `MOST_BYTES`; `feedback_targets` took 1.5 ms at depth 6, 11.8 ms at 7, 46 ms at 8, so depth 20 is effectively forever. A two-wide lattice of about 40 layers does the same without duplicates. `feedback_targets` runs for every sink in the input thread's `feedback()` (`crates/ctrlassist-device/src/thread.rs:595`), and the panic guard doesn't catch a hang, so every setup's virtual pads freeze. Fix: compute each node's `SourceSet` once, bottom-up along `order` (O(nodes × inputs)); optionally cap node count in `build`. **Severity: high. Verified** by probe timings.
- [x] **C-2. Node and sink counts are not capped.** Raised under C-1. About 2,500 `[sink.vN] input="a"` entries fit in 64 KiB, each a uinput virtual pad. The architecture's list of what building a graph rejects should probably gain a node cap and a sink cap. **Severity:** not given separately. **Suspected**: the reviewer did not trace the daemon.
- [x] **C-3. Thresholds that silently break a mode are accepted.** `crates/ctrlassist-core/src/mux.rs:131-145` (`Mux::validate`) checks only `0 ≤ release ≤ engage`, both finite. With `engage > 1` no button input ever becomes active on a group, so an owned group always falls back to input 0 (probe: priority mode with engage 1.5, release 0.5, the assist pressing South changed nothing), and loose buttons, combined with OR, disagree with grouped ones. With `release = 0` an input that touched a group once owns it forever (probe: after the assist moved and released the left stick, the primary pushing it to −0.9 left the virtual pad at 0). Both pass `Profile::check` in the full form. Fix: require `0 < release ≤ engage ≤ 1`, better checked in `profile.rs` so the error carries its location (C-6). **Severity: medium. Verified** by probe.
- [x] **C-4. A profile for a newer schema is misreported, and the form can quietly downgrade it.** `crates/ctrlassist-protocol/src/profile.rs:627-656` (`from_toml`): the `schema > SCHEMA` check runs after a `deny_unknown_fields` deserialize, so a newer file using any new key fails with "unknown field" and never reaches "this profile is for schema 2". Fix: read `schema` from a `toml::Table` first. `form.rs:317-324`: `form::from_toml` skips the `schema` key and accepts `schema = 2`, and `form::to_toml` writes the values back without it, so a client that opens a newer profile in its form and saves it rewrites it as schema 1: the "half-read" the architecture forbids. Fix: return `None` unless `schema` is absent or equal to `SCHEMA`. **Severity: medium. Verified** on the protocol side by probe; whether the window actually offers a form for such a profile is **suspected**.
- [x] **C-5. Uppercase hex in `serial:`, `port:` and `model:…:name` is accepted but never matches.** `crates/ctrlassist-protocol/src/profile.rs:424-435` keeps these as `Exact(text)` with case unchanged, while the device crate writes lowercase hex (`crates/ctrlassist-device/src/pad.rs:101-107`); bare addresses, `address:` and `model:V:P` are lowercased. `serial:054C:09CC:abc` parses and passes `check`, never matches, and the setup waits forever for a connected pad. Fix: lowercase the two hex fields only, not the serial, port or name. **Severity: low-medium. Verified** by probe.
- [x] **C-6. The core's refusals lose where the problem is.** `profile.rs:813-815`. A full-form mux with only `release = 0.2` (above the default engage of 0.1) is refused with `at = ""` and a generic message naming no node. A full-form `mux = "toggle"` with one input gets the default switch `Some(1)` and is refused for a switch the user never wrote. Fix: check both in `mux()` with `at`; for a one-input toggle, default the switch to input 0 or "any input". **Severity: low. Verified** by probe.
- [x] **C-7. A switch on a node that never selects swallows the button.** `crates/ctrlassist-core/src/mux.rs:236-252`, `demux.rs:110-118`. A configured switch is always taken out of the input, even on a priority or average mux with no switched rule, or a multicast demux; the game loses that button and pressing it does nothing. `profile.rs` accepts both. Fix: refuse a switch on a node that doesn't select. **Severity: low** (may be intended). **Verified** by reading.
- [x] **C-8. Conventions and dead code.** `ProfileError` (`profile.rs:454`) lacks `#[non_exhaustive]`, unlike the crate's other public errors. `Profile::to_toml` (`profile.rs:665`) has no caller outside tests, so the schema's serialize side exists only for a round-trip test. `form::to_toml` (`form.rs:305`) writes number and switch values unquoted: an `outputs` value of `"3\n[source.x]…"` goes straight into the TOML (very low risk: only a client's own values reach it, and the daemon re-checks). **Severity: low. Verified.**

### Device

Paths under `crates/ctrlassist-device/src/` unless given.

- [ ] **D-1. Writes to a USB pad's raw node block the input thread.** `dualsense.rs:481-528` (`RawOutput::write`, `rumble`, `pass`), reached from `thread.rs:615` (`pad.pass`), `:635` and `:649` (`pad.rumble`), `:448`, `:662`. On USB a hidraw `write()` is a synchronous transfer (`hidraw_write` → … → `usb_interrupt_msg(…, USB_CTRL_SET_TIMEOUT)`), waiting for the OUT endpoint's next interval and up to 5 s if the pad stalls; O_NONBLOCK doesn't apply. The kernel's own rumble path is asynchronous; Bluetooth only queues an skb, so it is fine. A game updating a virtual DualSense's lightbar every frame with two USB DualSenses behind it makes about 120 synchronous writes a second on the input thread, each stalling every setup's input for an endpoint interval (likely 1–4 ms, not confirmed); a pad wedged mid-unplug can freeze every setup for up to 5 s. Outside what `just bench` measures; D-4 doubles the writes. Fix: a one-slot "latest report" mailbox per pad, drained by a writer thread. **Severity: medium to high.** The code path is **verified**; the kernel side is from knowledge, and the stall's size is **suspected**, not measured.
- [ ] **D-2. Tap reports can crowd out the reports the control side depends on.** `thread.rs:1022-1029` and `977-981`. `Report::Tapped` shares the 64-slot `sync_channel` with `Stopped`, `Failed`, `PadGone`, `GraphTaken`, `GraphRefused` and `PadRefused`, sent with `try_send` and dropped when full; the Setup, Pad or NewGraph they carry is then freed on the input thread (a uhid destroy can wait on `cancel_work_sync`). Tapping is on whenever the daemon logs at debug (`crates/ctrlassist-daemon/src/lib.rs:239`); each handling adds up to 8 taps and one epoll batch holds 32 events, so 256 taps, while the normal-priority `reports` thread trails a SCHED_FIFO producer. Daemon consequences: a lost `Stopped` leaves the entry in `stopping` forever (the stop rpc never replies, tokens never restored, pads hidden until the daemon exits); a lost `GraphTaken` leaves `taking.sent` set (update and select refused as busy, `bring_back` blocked, the setup Held forever); a lost `Failed` hangs like a lost `Stopped`. Nothing resynchronizes. Fix: a separate bounded channel for taps, or reserve room for reports that carry ownership. Independently reported by the device review and the daemon review. **Severity: medium** (both). The queue mechanics are **verified** by reading and the reactor's lack of recovery as a design gap; an actual overflow is plausible, not observed.
- [ ] **D-3. Making a virtual DualSense can block forever.** `dualsense.rs:699-743` (`Emulator::create`) waits only for a bind, an event node or UHID_STOP, with `poll(.., None)`. If the descriptor copied into the state dir is corrupt (`is_whole()` checks only non-empty and at most 4096 bytes), hid-playstation's probe fails in `hid_parse` before `hid_hw_start`, so no START or STOP comes, and hid-generic declines a pad a special driver matches, so no bind comes either: the blocking worker hangs and the setup stays Starting. Not a case for a timeout: check the descriptor more strictly when loaded, or treat a missing UHID_START as refusal. **Severity: low to medium. Suspected**; the code is verified, the kernel behavior is from memory.
- [ ] **D-4. ## The hiding service without root

From the security review's inventory and the research on dropping root. Evidence labels from the research: **V** verified on the maintainer's machine (Linux 7.3.4, systemd 262, NixOS), **D** documented (source given), **U** unverified.

### Options considered

1. **A dedicated system user with a minimal capability set. Set aside on its own; kept only with option 3.** `AmbientCapabilities=CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE` covers rows 4, 8, 10, 11 and 12, `CAP_FOWNER` and `CAP_CHOWN` row 13, and nothing needs `CAP_SYS_ADMIN`. But it can't replace the reload (row 7, `ROOT_ONLY`; only a polkit rule or `CAP_KILL` would), and row 6 stays root-equivalent, as does handing the user `/run/udev/rules.d` through tmpfiles. `CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE` also opens every file not on a read-only mount and every node the cgroup allows; even with `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectKernelTunables=yes`, `DevicePolicy=closed` and `DeviceAllow=char-input rw`, `char-hidraw rw` (hidraw is major 244, V) it can open every keyboard's event node for keylogging and injection, close to root on a desktop. Cosmetic alone.
2. **Group or ACL access instead of capabilities. Kept, as part of the drop-root design.** A shipped static rule gives a hidden pad's nodes `GROUP="ctrlassist-hider"`, `MODE="0660"`, `TAG-="uaccess"`: only the service's group can open them, not the seat user nor members of `input` (the maintainer is in `input`, V). A group needs no capability where mode 0000 forces `CAP_DAC_OVERRIDE`. Keeping 0660 means a restore puts no mode back (row 13 goes for re-bound pads), since the stock rules set `GROUP="input"` on the fresh nodes the re-bind makes. Caveat: no-driver pads keep their nodes and any stale user ACL, which a group-rw mask leaves effective (0000 used to mask it); simplest fix, grab such pads instead of hiding them. A static rule gives PlayStation-driver hidraw nodes the service's group (harmless: the stock group is root). For bind and unbind, udev processes driver objects (`+drivers:hid:playstation` with `USEC_INITIALIZED`, V), so a rule `SUBSYSTEM=="drivers", KERNEL=="playstation|xpad|…", RUN+="chgrp … %S%p/bind %S%p/unbind"` plus `chmod g+w` opens row 8 to the group, as udev rules routinely do for sysfs attributes ([Arch backlight rules](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Backlight), D). The residual is unbinding any device from the listed drivers; listing `hid-generic` would include keyboards, local denial of service rather than escalation. Whether coldplug catches modules loaded at boot and by `modprobe` later is U. Set aside: tmpfiles `z` lines (don't follow later module loads) and tmpfiles ownership of `/run/udev/rules.d` (root-equivalent).
3. **No runtime rules: one static rule keyed on a marker the service toggles. Kept: the move that matters either way.** udev's `TEST` key substitutes before `stat()` (D, `TK_M_TEST` calls `apply_format_value` in [udev-rules.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/systemd/systemd/main/src/udev/udev-rules.c); `IMPORT{file}` too), though udev(7) here doesn't list `TEST` among the substituting fields (V), so it needs a spike. The shape, shipped at 72 as today: `SUBSYSTEM=="input", ATTRS{uniq}=="?*", TEST=="/run/ctrlassist/hide/%s{id/vendor}-%s{id/product}-%s{uniq}", GROUP="ctrlassist-hider", MODE="0660", TAG-="uaccess"`, with similar lines for serial-only and model-only, and a hidraw line using `IMPORT{parent}="HID_UNIQ"` and `$env{HID_UNIQ}` (`$attr` falls back to the parent `ATTRS` matched, D). It removes rows 6 and 7 (no reload, since `TEST` is evaluated at each event, which also ends the 3 s throttle race) and all rule text built from pad strings; the service writes only file names built from the same checked strings. A hostile `uniq` with `../` can only hide the attacker's own pad. Markers persist in `/run`, so a returning pad is hidden on `add` as today. Revoking access per pad without a re-bind isn't possible for the case that matters: `EVIOCREVOKE` and `HIDIOCREVOKE` revoke only the file they are called on and its dups (D, [HIDIOCREVOKE commit](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/b31c9d9dc343146b9f4ce67b4eee748c49296e99); in 6.12 per [LKML](https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2408.3/06558.html) and the [6.12-rc4 selftest](https://tangled.org/tjh.dev/kernel/blob/v6.12-rc4/tools/testing/selftests/hid/hidraw.c); present in 7.3 and 6.14.7 headers, V), so they can't touch Steam's own descriptor; `setfacl` needs the owner or `CAP_FOWNER`, leaves open descriptors alone, is lost on reconnect, and logind re-applies ACLs to `uaccess` nodes. No kernel requirement; small effort.
4. **Delegating to existing privileged brokers. Set aside.** logind's `TakeDevice` is for the session controller only, one per session (the compositor), and `TakeControl(force)` is root-only (D, [org.freedesktop.login1(5)](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/org.freedesktop.login1.html)); hidraw support arrived in systemd v257 ([PR 33970](https://github.com/systemd/systemd/pull/33970), D); it neither hides nor cuts off anyone's descriptors, and is unusable while KWin holds the session. polkit with a D-Bus-activated helper, or pkexec, is still root (and `allow_active` is weaker than TisVlay's seat rule without a custom rule; pkexec is setuid, worse). `systemd-run` and transient units: `manage-units` is about root. udevd's varlink is `ROOT_ONLY` with no trigger method. The one useful piece, a polkit rule letting the service user reload `systemd-udevd.service`, is made unnecessary by option 3.
5. **Kernel features that hide without privilege. Set aside: none does.** `EVIOCGRAB` is evdev only (hidraw has no grab). `EVIOCREVOKE`/`HIDIOCREVOKE` revoke only one's own file. hhd's `EVIOCREVOKEALL`/`JSIOCREVOKEALL` is a downstream patch (D, [hhd hide.py](https://github.com/hhd-dev/hhd/blob/master/src/hhd/controller/lib/hide.py)), absent from the 7.3 uapi headers (V). HID-BPF can drop reports before hidraw and input see them (D, [hid-bpf docs](https://docs.kernel.org/hid/hid-bpf.html)), but it mutes the daemon too and the pad stays listed, failing "absent, not silent"; loading needs `CAP_BPF` and `CAP_PERFMON` (D, [syscall.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/kernel/bpf/syscall.c)); a pinned map an unprivileged process controls is plausible, but getting its descriptor under `unprivileged_bpf_disabled=2` (V) is U; no mainline hook denies a hidraw open (the proposed BPF hook functions are absent from kallsyms, V; the merged commit made `hidraw_is_revoked` `static inline`, D). The `inhibited` sysfs attribute (`-rw-r--r-- root`, V) mutes input handlers, the daemon's included, and hidraw is a separate interface (D, [input-programming](https://docs.kernel.org/input/input-programming.html)). USB `authorized` (`-rw-r--r-- root`, V) disconnects the pad for everyone, USB only. Unbind without re-bind loses the pad for the daemon too. BPF LSM (in this machine's LSM list, V) could deny opens and reads on existing descriptors through `file_permission`, a kernel-enforced eviction with no re-bind, but needs root, `bpf` in the boot LSM list (varies by distribution) and a lot of work: not a non-root option.
6. **How comparable projects do it.** No project found hides from hidraw-reading Steam without root; CtrlAssist's re-bind is the only kernel-enforced eviction among them.

### The two candidate designs

**Drop root (D1).**

- The user `ctrlassist-hider` from `sysusers.d`, or `users.users` on NixOS; socket activation as today; `CapabilityBoundingSet=` empty; `NoNewPrivileges`.
- Sandbox: `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ReadWritePaths=/run/ctrlassist/hide`, `DevicePolicy=closed`, `DeviceAllow=char-input rw char-hidraw rw`.
- Static rules shipped with the package: option 3's marker rule setting the group as in option 2; the PlayStation hidraw group rule; the rule that opens bind and unbind to the group for a short driver list.
- The marker directory is owned by the service user through tmpfiles, not a plain `RuntimeDirectory`, which systemd deletes at stop and would lose the crash records.
- Code: the udev monitor replaces `settle`; no-driver pads are grabbed instead of hidden, removing rows 10 and 13.
- A compromise yields: opening any hidden pad or PlayStation hidraw node, re-binding the listed drivers (denial of service), hiding pads. Not root.
- Behavior: the hidraw case and evdev pads as today, and a crash too: markers persist until `ExecStopPost`'s reconcile, which runs as the same user, or until reboot.
- Portability: no kernel requirement. SELinux on Fedora (udev's `chgrp` on sysfs, its `stat` of `/run/ctrlassist`) is U; AppArmor doesn't confine udevd on Ubuntu.
- Effort: moderate, about two commits plus updating the VM check. The privileged code shrinks and the rule-text injection surface disappears.
- Possible refinement, confining re-bind power to pads only: the joystick event node's rule chgrps its HID parent's `uevent`; the service writes `change <uuid> CTRLASSIST=rebind`; a rule matching `ENV{SYNTH_ARG_CTRLASSIST}=="rebind"` does `ATTR{driver/unbind}="%k"` then `ATTR{subsystem/drivers_probe}="%k"`. Needs kernel 4.13 or later for synthetic uevent arguments.

**Keep a shrunk root (K1).**

- Option 3's static marker rule; no `rules.d` writes; no reload; the rule sets `MODE="0600"` owned by root instead of 0000.
- With uid 0 as owner, 0600 nodes, 0200 sysfs files and 0644 `uevent` files are all writable, so `CapabilityBoundingSet=` can probably be empty (U, by DAC reasoning).
- Sandbox: `ProtectKernelTunables=yes` plus `ReadWritePaths=/run/ctrlassist /sys/bus/hid/drivers /sys/bus/usb/drivers`, `DevicePolicy=closed` with `DeviceAllow`, `ProtectProc=invisible`.
- Effort: small, and a strict improvement even if D1 never ships. Caveat: uid 0 still owns most files on the system, which is why D1 is the cleaner end state.
- A cheap interim step even before K1: narrowing `ReadWritePaths=/run` to `/run/ctrlassist /run/udev/rules.d` and adding `DevicePolicy` closes the side routes, though not the `rules.d` one (H-11).

### Spikes

Each in the NixOS VM with fake pads.

1. **Marker rule.** Does a static rule using `TEST` with `%s{…}` substitution hide the event, js and hidraw nodes on the `add` after a re-bind and on a reconnect, with no reload? Does removing the marker and re-binding restore the `uaccess` ACL? Run on systemd 262 and on the oldest supported udev, since the man page doesn't promise substitution in `TEST`. The research named Debian 12 (252) and Ubuntu 22.04 (249); per the decisions, the floor may move to Ubuntu 26.04 if an older udev fails.
2. **Group instead of mode.** With `GROUP=ctrlassist-hider`, `MODE=0660` and the tag removed: can a seat user who is also in `input` open the node? Does a VT or user switch bring the ACL back? Can a capability-less service user open it and pass the descriptor?
3. **Unprivileged re-bind.** (a) Does a `SUBSYSTEM=="drivers"` chgrp/chmod rule take effect at coldplug and on `modprobe`, and let a group member re-bind `playstation` and `xpad`? (b) Does the re-bind triggered from a udev rule by a synthetic uevent work, without deadlock, and cut off an open hidraw reader, for USB HID, Bluetooth (uhid) and `xpad`?
4. **K1 sandbox.** Does `ReadWritePaths=` re-open `/sys/bus/*/drivers` under `ProtectKernelTunables=yes`? Does uid 0 with an empty bounding set still hide and restore?

### Open uncertainties

- The exact uid check behind `ROOT_ONLY`.
- Whether `TEST` substitution holds on older udev.
- Coldplug of driver objects in practice.
- SELinux behavior.
- Steam's own use of the SDL variable.
- Whether udevd re-reads changed rules on its own when an event arrives, which would make the reload unnecessary even with runtime rules (raised by the security review; the research found a 3 s-throttled check that is racy).
