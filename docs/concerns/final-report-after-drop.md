# A pad's last output report can land after the pad is given back

A pad given its raw node (a DualSense, a DualShock 4) has a writer thread of its own, so a pad that has stopped answering can't hold the input thread: a write to a raw node on USB can wait five seconds in the kernel. When the pad is dropped, the writer writes what is left, then a last report that leaves the pad at rest: its motors stopped, a DualSense's trigger effects off, and the lights its driver keeps. Whoever dropped the pad doesn't wait for that, so the write races what comes next, and where the pad was hidden, that is the hiding service re-binding its driver.

Every other pad that rumbles has such a thread too, which updates its one kernel effect: an update of an effect on a pad another program makes through uinput waits until that program answers, up to 30 seconds. When the pad is dropped, its event node goes to that thread with it: the thread sends what level is left, erases the effect, which the kernel stops first, and then closes the node, which ends the pad's grab. Closing it from the dropper instead could wait as long, since a grab is let go with a request on the node, held by an update or erase under way. So the grab of such a pad, whose maker has stopped answering, outlasts the drop by up to a minute and a half: an update under way, the erase, and the erase tried again as the node is closed, 30 seconds each.

**Risk.** Medium to low. On a pad that answers, the write takes about a millisecond and comes first. Where it loses to a re-bind, the write fails and the driver's re-bind sets the lights and stops the motors on its own, but never touches a DualSense's trigger effects: what a lost race leaves is a trigger that a game made stiff or vibrating staying so, which a player feels, and not only a rumble. A pad that was only grabbed gets no re-bind, and then nothing races the write. A pad another program makes is only ever grabbed, there being no driver to re-bind; one whose maker hangs stays hidden from other readers until its thread is through, and its maker, hung, isn't sending it anything to read.

**Mitigation.** The device crate says when a pad's writer is through. `Pad::rested` gives, before the pad is dropped, a handle whose `wait` blocks until the writer has left the pad at rest, and `Pad::let_go` drops the pad and gives the same; it can be sent to another thread, dropping it waits for nothing, and a pad with no writer is done already. For a pad given its raw node, it waits until the last report has been written, or refused as by a pad that has gone, and the node closed: the write the re-bind races, at most the kernel's five seconds on USB. For a pad with a kernel effect, it waits until the effect is erased, and not for the grab let go and the node closed after; the trigger effects being a raw node's, the restore needs to wait only for that kind, which the handle names. Waiting on drop in the reactor stays set aside, since a stalled pad on USB would hold every request for up to five seconds. The daemon doesn't use the handle yet: its stop still drops a setup and asks for the restore at once.

**Closes when.** The daemon, as it gives a pad back, takes the pad's handle before dropping the setup and waits on it off the reactor, on the blocking pool, before that pad's restore: the stop answers at once, and only that pad's restore follows its last write.
