# A Steam Controller is hidden only on its cable, and that is untried on the pad

A setup reads a Steam Controller (2026) pad through its raw node, which has the kernel's driver take the pad's gamepad and motion devices away for as long as it is open. Games that read event nodes, and SDL's other drivers, see nothing of the pad then, as of a pad grabbed. Two kinds of reader go by the raw node too: Steam, which opens the raw node of every Valve pad itself, and any game whose SDL3 reads the pad with its own driver for it (`SDL_hidapi_steam_triton.c`), which it does by default. Unless the pad is hidden they read it beside the setup's virtual pad, and a press arrives twice.

**On its cable the hiding service takes it,** by an exception the maintainer chose on 2026-11-26. The service refuses any pad with a keyboard or a pointer among its device's inputs, since the re-bind that hiding is would cut that off too; this pad's one HID device carries its lizard mode's keyboard and mouse beside its gamepad, and they are the pad's own, nobody's keyboard. So for `28de:1302` bound by `hid-steam` the service re-binds all the same, and the rule still hides neither the keyboard's node nor the pointer's: they stay as the stock rules make them, saying nothing while lizard mode is off. The service opens the pad's raw node for the daemon, which is the node of the client HID device the driver makes beside the pad's own. That device is made anew by each re-bind, under another name and with no unique string, so the one marker that can name its raw node is the pad's USB port and model together (`raw-<interface>-<HID_ID>`), which the service already writes for every HID pad on USB.

**Behind a Puck, over Bluetooth, and through uhid it is only grabbed** in effect: the service doesn't take the other products at all, and a pad made through uhid, as the tests' fake is, has no USB interface for the marker to name, so its gamepad and motion nodes are hidden and its raw node is not.

**Two holders at once,** where it isn't hidden. Such a reader doesn't only read: it writes the pad's settings as the daemon's writer does, on the same raw node. Steam turns the motion sensors off when it has no use for them, sets lizard mode as its own configuration says, and sends its own rumble and haptics; SDL3's driver sends lizard mode off every three seconds and its own rumble. The pad takes whichever came last, and the daemon sets the pad's settings once, as it takes it. So while Steam runs, motion behind a virtual DualSense may stop until the profile is started again; a game's rumble may be cut short or mixed with Steam's; and lizard mode may come back. And while Steam holds the raw node the driver doesn't make the pad's joystick again, so a pad Steam took first is not listed, and one a setup let go can't be taken again until Steam lets go too ([idea](../ideas/steam-controller-held-by-steam.md)).

**What is known.** In a virtual machine on Linux 7.3.4, against a uhid stand-in with the retail descriptor's keyboard and mouse: the service hides the pad, its marker made; its keyboard's node is left in the group `input` and unmarked; the service opens the raw node and the setup reads the pad through it; and the pad is shown again as the daemon stops. That the port-and-model marker names a real pad's raw node rests on the kernel's source, where the client device is given the pad's own parent, and has not been seen. That Steam opens the raw node, and that SDL3 reads it by default, is read in their sources and rules. Whether Steam honors its controller blacklist for its own pad, which the daemon writes for a pad only grabbed, is not known. What the grant of the raw node to the seat allows, and the race with another program's hold, are [their own concern](steam-raw-node-grant.md).

**Risk.** Medium on a cable, until seen: if the marker doesn't name the raw node, the hide fails and says so, the pad left as it was and grabbed instead. High behind a Puck or over Bluetooth for anyone who plays through Steam: a Steam game sees the real pad beside the virtual one, and the assist's help comes with the primary's every press twice. Low for games started outside Steam that read pads the usual way.

**Mitigation.** The window and the tray show which came of it, Hidden or Grabbed, and the tray says once when a profile that asked for Hidden has a Steam Controller only grabbed. A profile that asks for the pad to be left visible is refused. The user guide says what is seen and what can be done.

**Closes when** a pad on its cable is seen hidden from Steam and from an SDL3 game, and the maintainer has decided what a Puck and Bluetooth get. With a pad on its cable and the hiding service installed: run a profile of it at Hidden; `ls -l` its raw node (`ctrlassist pads` names the pad, `udevadm info` its nodes) and see the group `ctrlassist-hider` and no access for you; start Steam and look in its controller settings for one pad, the virtual one; run an SDL3 program that lists controllers (`testcontroller` from SDL3's tests) and count one; type in a text editor while pressing the pad's buttons and see nothing typed; stop the profile and see the pad back, typing and pointing again. With Steam started first, plug the pad in and see whether CtrlAssist lists it at all. Behind a Puck, with the profile at Hidden: look in Steam's controller settings for one pad or two, and restart Steam to see whether the blacklist entry the daemon wrote keeps it from listing the real pad.
