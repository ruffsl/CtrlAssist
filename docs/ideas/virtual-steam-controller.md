# A virtual Steam Controller

An identity that presents the setup's virtual pad as a Steam Controller (2026), as the DualSense identity presents one as a DualSense: made through uhid, bound by the kernel's `hid-steam`, and read by SDL and Steam as the real pad is. It would carry what no other identity can: both trackpads, the back buttons, the capacitive touches, and the pad's own haptics passed through to a real Steam Controller behind it.

**What others have shown.** Two projects present a virtual `28de:1302` that Steam lists and uses; one of them, `anderstvoss/virtualgamepad`, does it through Linux uhid, bound by `hid-steam` on 7.3 and read by SDL. Steam listed it once the stand-in answered its attributes (feature command `0x83`) with a real controller's, its serials (`0xAE`), its settings (`0x87`) and a few acknowledgments; neither project's captures show a signed challenge. The real pad's report descriptor, 372 bytes from a retail controller on a cable, and Steam's whole exchange with it, are published in `Nonary/libvirtualgamepad` (MIT). This project's own fake (`ctrlassist_test::FakeSteamController`) already answers what the kernel's driver asks, from a descriptor written here. A Puck can't be stood in for this way for SDL, which wants the USB interface numbers a uhid device hasn't, so the identity would be a pad on a cable.

**What Steam does with one.** It takes the pad into Steam Input, as it does a real one, and shows games a virtual pad of its own made through uinput: a game through Steam would read Steam's pad, configured by Steam, not CtrlAssist's. Steam offers a firmware update to a pad whose firmware it finds old, which the stand-in would have to decline or survive. And while Steam holds the virtual pad's raw node, the kernel's driver takes its gamepad away, so games that read event nodes see nothing of it.

**What it would take.** An emulator beside the DualSense one, in the device crate: the descriptor, either the real one's bytes, which are MIT-licensed and a fact about the hardware, or one written here from the driver's reading, as the test crate's fake is (a choice to make with the identity), answers to the driver's and Steam's feature requests, a state report written from the pad state with both touchpads, the paddles and the capacitive touches in their places, and its output reports, rumble and the haptic pulses, read back into the router and passed whole to a real Steam Controller behind it, as a virtual DualSense passes its output report to a real DualSense. Lizard mode would be the emulator's to answer and never to do.

**Why deferred.** Whether CtrlAssist wants what follows, Steam Input taking the pad over and a firmware update offered to something that isn't a pad, is the maintainer's to decide, and nobody here has a real pad to hold the stand-in to.

**Would bring it in.** The maintainer choosing it, once a real pad is on the desk to compare.
