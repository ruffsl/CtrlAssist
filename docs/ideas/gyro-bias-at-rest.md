# Measuring a pad's gyroscope bias at rest

A pad's gyroscope reads a little turning while it is still: its bias. A DualSense stores a bias in its calibration report, and on the pad the motion spike carried it was wrong. At rest that pad read 4.5, 6.7 and 0.2 counts where it stored 4, 15 and -5; taking the stored bias off, as SDL does, drifted 26° a minute one way, and taking none off, as the kernel's driver does, 24° a minute the other. A bias measured over 20 s at rest left under 1° a minute ([the plan](../plans/motion-and-touch.md)).

CtrlAssist could measure each pad's bias itself, whenever the pad lies still, and take it off before the rate reaches the pad state, so that a virtual pad drifts less than the pad read directly. At rest would be a state kept report by report on the input thread, with no timer and no wake: a report is still when each gyroscope axis is within a threshold of the run's mean and the accelerometer within one of its own; still reports add to the run, and a moving one ends it. Once a run spans long enough by the pad's own sensor clock, its mean is the bias, refined for as long as the run lasts; until a pad has lain still once, its stored bias stands. The thresholds tell a pad lying on a table (under 0.3°/s by the plan's research) from one in a hand (a tremor of 0.5 to 15°/s), so a held pad doesn't measure. A turn slower than the threshold, kept up longer than a run, would be learned as bias, the weakness of any calibration at rest. The shortest run that serves could be read from a spike record with rests in it, the bias of runs of 1, 2, 5 and 10 s against that of the whole 20 s.

The research's reason for it is a virtual pad whose motion changes source: a game that has learned one pad's bias drifts on another's, and two pads' stored biases differ (an inference, not seen).

**Why deferred.** The aim is parity with the real pad: a virtual DualSense carries the pad's readings with its own stored calibration applied, and drifts as the pad does read directly, which Steam and games already cope with; Steam measures at rest for itself. Measuring here would bring in a length of stillness, a number of seconds that nothing waits on but which is still a duration of CtrlAssist's own. And nobody has seen the drift step when one pad's motion gives way to another's.

**Would bring it in.** A visible jump in drift when an assist's gyroscope stands in for the primary's and when it goes back; or reports of drift that games don't correct.
